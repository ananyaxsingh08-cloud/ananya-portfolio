Project 01 Automotive · Thesis 2026

Drift — a dual-mode
SUV-Pickup concept for 2030.

A future-focused transformable mobility concept that seamlessly shifts between SUV and pickup configurations — adaptive in utility, bold in identity, and envisioned for the evolving demands of 2030 mobility.

04 · Abstract

One vehicle. Two states. Zero compromise.

By 2030, the line between work vehicle and lifestyle vehicle will dissolve. Drift is a thesis investigation into what happens when an SUV refuses to choose — seamlessly transforming into a pickup without surrendering its identity.

A design proposition for Tata Technologies' 2030 mobility roadmap: a single architecture that serves the city on Monday and the trail on Saturday.

Transformable SUV Pickup EV-Ready 2030 Lifestyle Faceted Form Adaptive

The brief, refined.

How can a future-focused SUV for 2030 be designed as a transformable mobility solution — that seamlessly adapts between passenger and utility functions, while expressing a bold, sporty, and authoritative visual identity?

01 · Stance reads first
Bold, confident SUV stance signals safety, power, reliability.

EVs lose the grille — buyers now read fascia geometry, surfaces, proportions instead.

02 · Utility, no compromise
Customers want large boot space, easy load/unload, rugged-yet-refined interiors.

Rising appetite for panoramic roofs, modularity, dynamic personalisation.

03 · Lifestyle > Spec
Vehicle choice is driven by lifestyle compatibility, not horsepower.

Camping, gigs, trekking, off-roading — users want one vehicle that supports all.

04 · Emotional appeal
Futuristic styling makes users feel modern, adventurous, capable.

Confident front stance and expressive surfaces create instant connection.

Showroom Synthesis

Customers aren't reading spec sheets first. They are reading the stance — deciding on the spot whether the vehicle understands them.

13.2 / 13.3 · Concept Directions

Forge vs Drift. The users choose.

Forge

Concept Direction 01

A rugged, honest silhouette inspired by expedition tools — upright glass, square wheel arches, exposed functional details. Mechanical honesty over aerodynamic sleekness.

Drift

Concept Direction 02 · Selected

A forward-leaning coupe-SUV silhouette with faceted panels — monolithic volumes meeting at sharp geometric transitions. A sportsback SUV inspired by the panther, frozen mid-step.

Concept voting · Verdict

Drift wins the brief. Across six of seven evaluated aspects, Drift outperformed Forge — driven by its surface language, EV identity, and emotional appeal. Forge was stronger on pure rugged capability cues, but that strength did not outweigh the cohesive design win of the sportsback coupe SUV direction.

"Drift feels future-ready; Forge feels like something I've seen before."
"The transformation in Drift looks designed-in, not added-on."
"Forge is more honest, but Drift is more exciting."
"Drift's stance makes it look fast even when parked."

17.3 · Closing thoughts

This thesis argued that the next decade of SUV design will be decided not by who adds more screens or more range — but by how the vehicle can adapt to the life the owner is actually living. DRIFT is one answer: a single architecture, two honest modes, one coherent identity. A vehicle that doesn't ask the owner to compromise on who they are today, or who they want to be tomorrow.

Primary research

Ergonomic challenges

Workers used pits instead of sliding equipment, causing strain and safety risks.

Spare part constraints

High tire costs limited spare availability, affecting maintenance efficiency.

Quality control issues

Manual sample checking was time-consuming, needing process optimisation.

R&D limitations

Innovation efforts faced hurdles in enhancing in-house manufacturing. The most expensive part is tyres — Rs 15,000 for 1 tyre, due to which buses don't keep a spare tyre. The workshop generally orders bus parts from Ashok Leyland or Tata (OEM) original equipment products.

Secondary research

The working table in the Dadar bus workshop is designed to assist in the repair and maintenance of vehicle components. It serves as a stable and organised surface where mechanics and technicians can disassemble, inspect, clean, and reassemble various mechanical parts of buses.

  • MakeCustom-made, likely fabricated in-house or by a local manufacturer
  • CategoryIndustrial workshop equipment
  • MaterialCast iron with a rotary mechanism
  • WeightEstimated around 50–70 kg (varies by steel gauge and mechanism)
  • ManufacturerBEST's engineering division or a local supplier
  • UsageDisassembling or assembling circular components, such as bus wheel hubs, brake drums, or gear assemblies
23 · Product Breakdown

What the old table does and doesn't do.

23.1 · Annotated breakdownExisting equipment

Side supports & brackets

Help in securing larger components. May cause discomfort if sharp edges are exposed.

Rotating gear mechanism

Allows workers to rotate the part for better access. Helps in minimising effort while working on heavy components.

Metal frame

Provides structural stability. Prevents vibrations from affecting precision work.

Blue trays on top

Used for collecting parts and preventing them from rolling off. However, debris accumulation is an issue (needs better cleaning access).

Gap Identification

Four shortcomings.

01
Cleaning difficulty

The blue trays accumulate debris, making maintenance harder. Workers may struggle with frequent cleanups.

"Cleaning takes time, and the dust accumulates fast."

02
Limited ergonomics

The table height and positioning might cause discomfort if not adjustable. Workers bending over for long hours may face back strain.

"Rotating is tiring after a while."

03
Lack of storage for tools

No visible dedicated storage for tools, forcing workers to place them elsewhere. Adding side compartments could improve efficiency.

"A small shelf for tools would help."

04
Manual rotation mechanism

Requires manual effort; could be improved with a motorised or foot-controlled system.

"Edges feel rough and can hurt."

What we captured well

  1. 01 Lighting issues, tool placement, table tilt difficulty.
  2. 02 Practical suggestions — adjustable light, easier table tilt.

Emerging themes

  1. 01 Tool distancing, table sharing / cart accessory.
  2. 02 Ergonomic issues (manual tilt).
  3. 03 Environment adaptability (seasonal lighting).

What could be improved

  1. 01 Frustrating issues — manual rotation.
  2. 02 Efficiency impact scale is 8 (10 being highest, 1 being lowest).
  3. 03 Limited user diversity (feedback might not cover all user types).

Directions to explore

  1. 01 Add adjustable or smart lighting.
  2. 02 Bring tools closer or integrate holders.
  3. 03 Simplify tilt mechanism (manual assist or automation).
  4. 04 Explore split-table or flexible design for multiple users.

Project 03 CMF Strategy 2025

Marshall — an elegant and timeless CMF strategy for an iconic identity.

An elegant and timeless CMF strategy designed for resilience within Marshall's iconic identity. Balancing heritage cues — leatherette, brass, script — with materials engineered to age beautifully and signal contemporary craft.

28 · Brand Study

A heritage rooted in rock & roll.

Marshall is a heritage-driven brand rooted in rock & roll culture, known for its bold, authentic, and powerful personality. Its products embody rugged durability, raw energy, and iconic design cues — from textured leatherette and metal hardware to the instantly recognisable script logo.

Balancing tradition with modern innovation, Marshall appeals to users who value authentic sound, expressive style, and a sense of identity that connects them to music's rebellious spirit.

Iconic Timeless Rugged Modern Bold Authentic Luxurious Vintage
User psychology insights
Musicians

Musicians (amateurs to pros): They value durability, authenticity, quality, tone. Visual plus tactile cues (knobs, switches, analog meters) matter.

Lifestyle audio

Those who buy speakers or headphones for both use & show. They want something that looks cool, aligns with their identity, and evokes rock culture.

Status & symbolism

Marshall products often signal "I'm into real sound / rock / tradition." Colour and materials that reinforce premium, classic, high-value craftsmanship help.

Tactility & sensory feedback

Materials that feel robust — knobs, metal weight, fabrics, leatherette. Finishes that age well (patina) can appeal.

Novelty & personal expression

While tradition is valued, users also like limited editions, colour variants, new textures to express individuality.

Embedded Logic · ESP32

// Pin definitions — Meemee electronic bidet · ESP32
#define OLED_RESET     -1   // OLED reset pin not used
Adafruit_SSD1306 display(128, 64, &Wire, OLED_RESET);

#define HEATER_RELAY_PIN  5   // GPIO for controlling heater relay
#define PUMP_RELAY_PIN   18   // GPIO for controlling pump relay
#define TEMP_SENSOR_PIN  16   // Analog pin for NTC thermistor
#define BUTTON_PIN       17   // GPIO for push button
#define LED_HEATER_PIN   25   // GPIO for Heater LED (simulated)
#define LED_PUMP_PIN     26   // GPIO for Pump LED (simulated)

void setup() {
  // Initialise serial communication
  Serial.begin(115200);

  // Initialise relays, button, and LEDs
  pinMode(HEATER_RELAY_PIN, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(PUMP_RELAY_PIN, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(LED_HEATER_PIN, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(LED_PUMP_PIN, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(BUTTON_PIN, INPUT_PULLUP);  // Use internal pull-up for button

  // Initialise OLED display (optional)
  if (!display.begin(SSD1306_I2C_ADDRESS, 0x3C)) {
    Serial.println("OLED allocation failed");
    while (true);  // Halt if the display does not initialise
